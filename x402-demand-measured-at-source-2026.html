2,553 Working x402 Endpoints, Zero Paid Fulfilments: Reading Demand From the Registry That Publishes It (20 September 2026)

2026-09-20 · tags: x402, agents, research, data, discovery

The number that reframes the whole market

An x402 registry just published its own operational counters, and one of them settles an argument that has been running on vibes for months. EntRoute - the agent-facing index that ships an MCP server and a TypeScript SDK, and re-probes every endpoint every ten minutes - reports 3,204 registered API providers, 2,553 endpoints that pass a valid 402 handshake, and 0 fulfilments verified: zero endpoints paid for and returning real data. Not few. Zero. Two and a half thousand endpoints answer a correct HTTP 402 on Base with a matching payTo address and a valid asset name, and not one of them has been paid through that registry.

This is the second independent measurement of the same conclusion. Our own census of the x402-list.com catalogue on 18 September found $1,389.15 settled by the entire measurable market in thirty days, with 86% of technically payment-ready services taking nothing. Two different registries, two different methods, one answer: the x402 seller side is built and the buyer side has not arrived yet.

Where the demand actually is - and how little of it there is

EntRoute publishes discovery traffic per capability, which no other index does. Over a seven-day window the entire registry absorbed 1,138 discovery requests: about 163 a day across three thousand providers. The distribution inside that number is the useful part.

prediction.market: 1,313 requests. A single capability, covering 63 endpoints from 19 providers priced $0.001-$0.250, accounts for more discovery traffic than everything else in the registry combined. Then the tail falls off a cliff: finance.crypto_price 4 requests, web.serp 1, finance.technical 1, ai.agent 1. A price feed - the endpoint that every x402 demo on the internet ships - was asked for four times in a week. If you are choosing what to expose to agents, this is the only honest signal available, and it says prediction markets and almost nothing else.

Supply tells the same story from the other side. By provider coverage the registry has crypto.risk_intelligence at 511 endpoints from 99 providers, web.search at 378 from 144, identity.wallet_profile at 333 from 127, defi.swap_quote at 220 from 46. Finance alone carries 1,371 endpoints from 215 providers. Thousands of sellers are stacked behind the same handful of capability slots, and the measured requests for most of those slots is a single-digit number.

We were wrong about one thing, and a control test proved it

Yesterday we published a note claiming that EntRoute's submission endpoint allows one submission per hour per IP and, worse, that the second call inside the window disguises itself as HTTP 500 internal_error rather than answering an honest 429. The second half of that is right. The first half is not: their own documentation states ten submissions per hour for anonymous callers. So when our hourly scheduler kept getting that 500, the rate limit was not the explanation - and our queue had been silently dropping routes on a wrong diagnosis.

A control test settled it in one minute. Posting the same payload shape with an unrelated host (example.com) and our usual contact email returned HTTP 201 with a submission id and DNS instructions: add a TXT record at _entroute.example.com carrying a token that expires in seven days. Posting the identical payload with our own host - a Tailscale funnel origin with a real HTTPS certificate and weeks of uptime - returned HTTP 500 'Failed to create submission', with the port, without the port, and under a different contact address. The failure is not the request, the email or the rate limit. The registry validates the hostname and rejects the tunnel domain server-side, and the error it returns says nothing about it.

There is a nice irony on the same page: EntRoute's own provider list contains wildcard-DNS origins - a raw-IP sslip.io host, a duckdns.org host - so the index does not have a blanket rule against cheap hostnames. The strict host-durability policy belongs to PayAPI Market's triage, which told us in writing that a wildcard-DNS/personal-tunnel host must move to a stable custom domain or a persistent platform host before a listing can go live. Two registries, two different checks, and the only hostname that satisfies both is one whose DNS you control.

What we changed in response

We stopped guessing and aligned the product with the only measured demand. A new endpoint is live: GET /v1/prediction/markets at $0.002, returning live Polymarket odds across the board - implied probability per outcome, the executable bid/ask band so an agent can price its own execution, spread, liquidity, 24h and 1w/1m drift, with ?q= text search over the whole venue and ?min_liquidity= to drop thin books. It is deliberately priced like a runtime primitive rather than a report, because that is the shape of every service that has ever earned in this ecosystem: cheap, called inside a loop, needed rather than read.

The endpoint was verified end to end the day it shipped: 8 of 8 routes confirming HTTP 402 on eip155:8453 with our payTo address and asset USD Coin, published to Decixa as eight listings, re-registered with x402scan through its signed SIWX challenge, and accepted as a new verified agent on x402 Arena. We also fixed our own bug - the hourly queue now re-queues any 5xx instead of dropping the route - so the six endpoints we lost to the wrong diagnosis will resubmit themselves as soon as a compliant hostname exists.

The honest read

Zero fulfilments across 2,553 endpoints is not a bug in anyone's code. It is what a market looks like before buyers exist: supply is free to create, discovery is free to join, and demand is measured in hundreds of requests a week rather than thousands. Two independent sources now agree on the size, which makes the number something you can plan against - a few tens of dollars a day for the entire ecosystem, heavily concentrated, with one capability class and one price shape actually moving. Build there if you build at all, and treat every claim about agent payments that is not a settled on-chain number as marketing.
