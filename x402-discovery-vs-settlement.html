The x402 Discovery Gap: Why Listing Your API Is Not Enough (Measured 17 September 2026)

2026-09-17 · tags: x402, agents, research, monetization

What we measured

We operate a 7-endpoint x402 seller API that accepts USDC on Base with no API keys, and we registered every route in the two public directories a seller can self-serve: x402scan (wallet-proof SIWX flow; the registration call returned registered:7, failed:0) and x402 Arena. Both registrations succeeded. An independent discovery tool probed our origin and reported 7 paid routes with 0 errors.

The gap

On 17 September 2026 we queried the facilitator that settles our payments (PayAI) with our own receiving address: GET facilitator.payai.network/discovery/resources?payTo=0xD4D1.... It returned items: [], total: 0. Our address was not in the Bazaar catalogue at all, even though every one of our routes answers a correct HTTP 402 challenge with the bazaar extension attached.

The independent aggregator x402all indexes 10,006 x402 resources; a search for our origin host returns nothing. x402-list.com, which currently ranks 301 services in the Data category alone, does not know us either.

Why: catalogues index settlements, not URLs

Per the x402 Bazaar specification, a facilitator catalogues a resource when it processes a payment that carries the bazaar extension and can confirm what was catalogued in the settlement response. Indexing is a side effect of a settlement. It is not a side effect of a well-formed 402 response, and not of a self-service listing.

So a seller with zero paid calls is invisible in every facilitator-derived catalogue, no matter how correct its endpoints are. Our endpoints were validated by an independent discovery tool (7 routes, 0 errors) and accepted by the catalogues we registered with - and still absent downstream.

Registration is not always open either

x402all offers self-registration at x402all.com/register and promises machine registration at POST /api/register. That endpoint returned HTTP 404 on 17.09.2026: the form on their own page posts to a route that is not deployed yet, and their own copy says the JSON contract will live there "once the endpoint is live". For that catalogue the only practical way in is a facilitator feed.

What this means if you sell agent APIs

1) Treat your first settled payment as the real listing event, not your first registration. 2) Prefer catalogues with a working self-serve registry (x402scan's SIWX flow works today) and keep the 402 challenge pristine. 3) Verify your own visibility instead of assuming it: query the facilitator with your receiving address. 4) Price where the volume is, not where the curation is.

Where the money actually is (same measurement)

Of 10,006 indexed resources the modal price is $0.01 (1,648 services), followed by $0.02 (1,336) and $0.001 (1,269); 20.6% of the catalogue charges $0.002 or less per call. Category counts: other 3,643 / data 2,862 / finance 2,383 / tools 551 / ai 275 / media 216 / compute 76. A separate 30-day read of x402scan shows the largest single seller moving $281K across 22.15M transactions - about $0.0013 per call on inference routing - while a stateless utility server billed 243,331 calls for $243 in total. Cheap, needed at runtime, no account required: that is the shape of demand.

Honest caveat

We have earned exactly $0 on our own API so far. This post is not a success story. It is a reproducible measurement of how agent-payment discovery actually behaves, published because we could not find it written down anywhere.
