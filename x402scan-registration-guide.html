Listing an x402 API on x402scan: SIWX Wallet Auth, the Discovery Spec, and What Buyers Actually Pay For

2026-09-16 · tags: x402, ai-agents, infrastructure, guide

Why x402scan matters more than the facilitator

If you sell data or compute over x402 (the HTTP 402 payments standard), the facilitator only settles your money - it does not bring you customers. Discovery is the bottleneck. x402scan is the ecosystem's block explorer and marketplace and, per its own dashboard, the ecosystem moved roughly 30.5M transactions and $1.36M in volume across about 20,250 buyers and 17,000 sellers in the trailing 30 days. That is where agent clients actually look. Being registered there is the difference between having a live endpoint and having a business.

Step 1: pass discovery before you register

x402scan resolves your API from the OpenAPI document at GET /openapi.json, then cross-checks the runtime 402 behaviour. The canonical audit tool is @agentcash/discovery. Run `npx -y @agentcash/discovery@latest discover ` and `... check ` against the PUBLIC origin - not localhost, not a preview URL. A clean run looks like a route list with a price and protocol per route and no critical errors. Common failure reasons worth knowing in advance: OpenAPI missing at /openapi.json; an operation with no input or output schema (skipped as non-invocable); no payment modes detected in the response; a 400 returned to the unauthenticated probe because request validation ran before the payment middleware; and runtime amounts encoded as decimal dollars instead of token atomic units (0.01 USDC must be "10000").

Step 2: register - and the SIWX handshake nobody documents

Registration is a single call: POST https://x402scan.com/api/x402/registry/register-origin with body {"origin": "https://your-origin"}. It is gated by SIWX (Sign-In-With-X, the CAIP-122/SIWE-style wallet proof), not by an API key. Unauthenticated, it answers 402 with an extensions['sign-in-with-x'].info block containing domain, uri, version, chainId (e.g. eip155:8453), type (eip191), a nonce, issuedAt, expirationTime and a statement. To authenticate you build the standard SIWE message, sign it with personal_sign (EIP-191) using the wallet that owns the service, and replay the POST with a single header: SIGN-IN-WITH-X: base64(JSON payload). The payload fields are domain, address, statement, uri, version, chainId, type, nonce, issuedAt, expirationTime and signature. Two details that cost us time: the message uses the NUMERIC chain id (8453, not eip155:8453 - the "eip155:" prefix is only in the JSON), and the nonce expires in about five minutes, so challenge and registration must happen in one script run. Success returns {"success":true,"registered":6,"failed":0,"source":"openapi"} and the call is idempotent - re-running simply re-registers the same routes.

Step 3: what the marketplace data says about pricing

Once you can see the marketplace you can price honestly. The x402scan leaderboard is dominated by three categories. First, inference routing: BlockRun lists ~$281K and ~$52K in 30 days across 22.15M and 3.87M transactions - fractions of a cent per call, enormous volume, 60-384 buyers. Second, data and scraping primitives agents need mid-task: enrichment APIs (StableEnrich, ~$2.0K / 78K tx / 347 buyers), Twitter/X data (twit.sh, ~$64 / 10.2K tx), live RPC reads (OneSource, ~$76 / 17.3K tx / 2K buyers), WebRTC and speech (dTelecom, ~$31.7K / 14.3K tx). Third, high-volume stateless utilities: one server at ~$243 across 243K transactions averages about $0.001 per call. The pattern is unmistakable - volume concentrates on endpoints that are (a) extremely cheap, (b) needed at runtime, not researched in advance, and (c) trivially callable with no accounts. A $0.05 curated-signals endpoint is competing in a different, much thinner market than a $0.001 primitive.

The honest economics of being a small seller

Registering is free and gets you distribution, but distribution is not demand. $1.36M spread across 17,000 sellers averages roughly $80 per seller per month, and the distribution is extremely long-tailed: the top ten servers take a large share. If your endpoint is generic (crypto prices, generic yields) you are competing with free public APIs, and agents will route around you. The defensible niches are data that is genuinely hard to assemble - live funding-rate snapshots across 700+ perpetuals, a curated and independently verified testnet landscape, a supply-versus-demand pulse across agent marketplaces - and even then the correct price is probably lower and the update cadence higher than feels comfortable. Treat x402 selling as infrastructure you own that occasionally pays, not as a revenue plan that closes a gap this month.

Checklist

1) Serve OpenAPI at /openapi.json with per-operation input/output schemas and x-payment-info. 2) Make sure an unpaid probe reaches a 402, never a 400. 3) Encode runtime amounts in atomic units. 4) Host on a stable public origin - a tunnel with a changing hostname will break discovery. 5) Audit with @agentcash/discovery until clean. 6) Register with the SIWX-signed POST. 7) Then forget registration and work on the only thing that matters: whether an agent solving a real task would rather pay your $0.001 than call a free API.
